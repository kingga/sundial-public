Sundial Privacy Policy

Effective date: 28 November 2026

Sundial is made by Isaac Matthew Skelton, an independent developer in New Zealand. Contact: contact.sundial.app@gmail.com.

The short version: Sundial keeps everything on your device. There are no accounts, no analytics and no advertising, and Sundial doesn't send your events or photos to anyone. If you buy Sundial Pro, or start its free trial on iPhone, Apple or Google handle the purchase, and Sundial never sees your payment details.

What Sundial keeps on your device

For each event: its title, emoji, colour, date (and for a timed event its time and time zone), whether it counts down or up, whether Reminders are on and which Early Reminder you chose, whether and when you archived it, an identifier, and a reference to its photo if it has one. These are stored in a database inside the app.

Sundial also keeps a note of whether you have Sundial Pro (see below), and whether it has asked for permission to send notifications, so it asks only once. If you start the 3-day Trial, it notes when that was (see below). If you dismiss the tip about adding a widget, or on iPhone the banner saying your Trial has ended (tapping the banner dismisses it too), it notes that, so neither shows again.

Sundial reads your device's time zone to work out "today" and to show dates correctly. It never asks for your location.

On iPhone, when you choose an event's emoji, Sundial checks whether your device's emoji keyboard is turned on, so it can open the keyboard there. It doesn't keep or send what it finds.

Photos

You choose a photo with your device's own photo picker; on iPhone this doesn't give Sundial access to your library. Sundial makes two resized copies on your device, one for the app and one for its widgets, and removes the photo's metadata (such as the camera and location) from both. Your original photo isn't changed or deleted, and nothing is uploaded.

Sundial Pro

Sundial Pro is bought through Apple's App Store or Google Play, under their terms and privacy policies. You pay Apple or Google, not the developer: your payment details never reach Sundial or the developer, and Sundial doesn't send anything about your purchase to the developer or anyone else.

On iPhone you can also try Pro free for 3 days. The 3-day Trial is a free purchase through Apple's App Store, under your Apple ID, which you choose to make. Android has no Trial.

To know whether you have Pro, and on iPhone whether you've started the Trial, Sundial asks the App Store or Google Play on your device. It keeps a note on your device that you have Pro, and one of when your Trial started, so both work offline. Those two notes are all Sundial keeps about your purchases. Restore Purchases asks the store again, only when you tap it.

Apple and Google give the developer sales and refund reports as part of running their stores, as described in their own policies.

Reminders

For an event that counts down, Sundial can show Reminders: a notification on the day and, if you choose one, an Early Reminder before it. You turn them on or off for each event with Remind me.

Sundial works out each Reminder and its wording on your device, and hands it to your device's own notification system to show at the right time. Reminders aren't sent through a push notification service or any server, and nothing about them leaves your device. Until they show, your device keeps the upcoming ones, with their wording, for Sundial. A Reminder shows the event's emoji and title, so, depending on your notification settings, others may see it on your lock screen.

Sundial asks for permission to send notifications once: the first time you save an event that counts down with Remind me on, never when the app opens. If you say no, it won't ask again; the editor shows "Notifications are off for Sundial" with a link to Settings, and you can turn notifications on or off there at any time.

Widgets and permissions

To show your events on the home screen, Sundial shares a copy of every event (archived ones included) and its widget photo with its widgets, using storage your device provides for an app and its widgets. On Android, Sundial also remembers which event each widget shows. Anyone who can see your home screen can see what your widgets show.

To decide whether to show its tip about adding a widget, Sundial asks your device whether one of its widgets is on your home screen, and on iPhone reads the system version so the steps match it. It doesn't keep either answer.

On Android, Sundial asks for permission to send notifications, for Reminders (Android 13 and later; earlier versions don't ask). It may also ask for Alarms & reminders access when you place a widget for a timed event: this helps widgets for timed events change on time and Reminders arrive on time. Without it, Android may show a Reminder up to about an hour late. You can turn either off in Settings. Sundial also uses standard permissions Android needs to update widgets and Reminders in the background, such as after your device restarts, and Google Play's billing permission, which lets you buy Pro through Google Play. Sundial for Android doesn't have permission to use the internet. On iPhone, the only permission Sundial asks for is to send notifications, for Reminders.

What leaves your device

Sundial doesn't use accounts, cloud storage, analytics, crash reporting or advertising, and doesn't send your events, your photos, your Reminders or anything about your purchases to the developer or anyone else. Buying or restoring Pro, and starting the Trial, go through the App Store or Google Play, as described above.

Your device's backups are the exception. Android's backup and phone-to-phone transfer, iCloud Backup, and backups to a computer can include Sundial's events, photos and the notes it keeps, described above, depending on your settings. Those backups are handled by Google or Apple under their own privacy policies.

If you email support, we receive what you choose to send, use it only to help you, and keep it only as long as needed for that and any legal obligations.

The online copy of these pages is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you visit it, GitHub logs and stores your IP address for security purposes, under the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Keeping and deleting

Your events stay on your device until you delete them. Archiving isn't deleting: archived events stay in Archived until you restore or delete them.

When you delete an event, Undo is offered for a few seconds; after that the event and its photos are deleted. Deleting or archiving an event, or turning off Remind me, cancels its upcoming Reminders. A photo you replace or remove is deleted when you save the event. Any files left behind are cleaned up the next time Sundial opens.

Sundial has no export or "delete everything" option yet: you can delete events one at a time, or delete the app, which removes its data from your device (but not from existing backups).

Your rights

You can ask for access to, or correction of, any personal information we hold about you (in practice, only support emails) by writing to contact.sundial.app@gmail.com. We can't see or retrieve the events on your device.

If you have a concern, you can contact the New Zealand Office of the Privacy Commissioner, or the data protection authority where you live. This policy doesn't limit any rights you have under the law where you live.

Changes

We'll update this policy, and its effective date, before releasing any change to how Sundial handles your information.
